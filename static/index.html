      Tobacco Disease Guide

      Common diseases affecting tobacco crops, identification, and prevention strategies.

      Alternaria Alternata (Brown Spot)

      Brown spot appears as circular spots on the lower leaves, gradually moving upwards. It is favored by warm, humid conditions and can cause significant yield loss if not managed.

      Management

      • Early reaping to remove infected lower leaves.
      • Proper curing and avoiding excessive nitrogen fertilizer.
      • Apply registered fungicides when symptoms first appear.

      Cercospora Nicotianae (Frog Eye Spot)

      Characterized by small, circular spots with white or light tan centers resembling frog eyes. It commonly occurs in wet weather and can lower leaf quality.

      Management

      • Maintain strict seedbed hygiene.
      • Destroy crop residues after harvest.
      • Ensure proper plant spacing for air circulation.

      Tobacco Mosaic Virus (TMV)

      Causes mottling and discoloration of leaves (a mosaic pattern). It is highly contagious and usually spread through mechanical transmission (hands, tools).

      Management

      • Wash hands thoroughly with soap and water before handling plants.
      • Do not smoke or use tobacco products while in the field.
      • Use TMV-resistant tobacco varieties if available.

      Angular Leaf Spot (Pseudomonas syringae)

      A bacterial disease causing dark, angular spots on leaves, often surrounded by a yellow halo. Spread is favored by driving rain and wind.

      Management

      • Avoid working in the field when plants are wet.
      • Rotate crops and bury or burn infected stalks.
      • Apply copper-based bactericides preventatively in high-risk areas.
